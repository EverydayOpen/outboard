Big folders, moved alongside.

Outboard finds the big folders apps keep on your Mac and, for the apps where the method is known, moves them to an external SSD: copied, compared file by file, the original kept until you say so, every step logged.

While it is running, it tells you when the drive is unplugged and parks a note where the folder was.

How a move works.

Each line is something the app does and checks, and each is in a log you can read.

  1. {{eduS1}}

    Every file is compared by size and SHA-256. One difference stops the move, and your Mac stays as it was.

    copy · compare

  2. {{eduS2}}

    The app's own setting is pointed at the copy, or a link is put where the folder was.

    switch

  3. {{eduS3}}

    The folder is renamed name.before-move. Until you confirm, you can roll back.

    .before-move

  4. {{eduS4}}

    Each step is written to a log before it happens. If the log can't be written, nothing moves.

    journal

After you confirm, the original goes to the Trash. Space comes back when you empty it. The whole sequence, and what Outboard reads and never does.

  • The link points at nothing from the moment the drive disappears until the guard parks it: seconds while Outboard runs, and until the next launch when it doesn't.
  • Apps that had a file open on the drive probably need a restart after it returns.

02 · Questions

Before you try it.

Short answers. The long ones are in the guides.

Does this make my drive work like internal storage?

No. Outboard moves the folders it knows to the drive and points the app at them. The app still runs from your Mac, and a drive on a cable is not the Mac's own storage. Outboard says nothing about speed.

What happens if I unplug the drive?

While Outboard is running it parks a note where the folder was and says so in its window and menu bar. When the drive is back it checks it and puts things back. It can't stop you unplugging a drive or make that harmless. The long answer.

Why doesn't Outboard move my Photos library itself?

Photos moves its own library, and Apple documents the steps. Outboard shows them, opens Photos and checks your drive first. Apple's page on moving a library mentions Photos creating a new empty library, which is why this stays Photos' own move.

Why does it refuse my drive?

Outboard refuses a network volume, a drive formatted FAT or exFAT (apps need APFS), the Time Machine disk, a disk image and, for the moves it does itself, a spinning hard disk. Each refusal shows its reason. Changing a format erases the drive, so Outboard never does it; it shows the Disk Utility steps.

Is it really free?

Yes. MIT licence, no account, no trial, no paid tier. The source is on GitHub.

Why is the app unsigned?

Until there is a Developer ID, public builds are unsigned betas, so macOS asks for one extra step the first time you open it. The download page shows it.

What does "Not yet tried on a real Mac" mean?

Nobody has run Outboard on a real Mac with a real external drive yet. Every move that changes anything stays hidden in the app until a named tester has run it and the public verify log says so.
